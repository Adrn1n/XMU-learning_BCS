# 今天课后作业
什么是情绪？情绪的认知心理和神经机制分别是什么？查阅相关文献，列出当前3个基于脑电信号的情绪识别算法及其原理。在此基础上，给出其中一个算法的实验结果，并试着提出改进思路和方法。
参考：[https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10264207/authors#authors](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/10264207/authors#authors)
   [https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9154557](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/abstract/document/9154557)

---

Emotion is a complex, multi-dimensional psychological and physiological response to internal or external stimuli, involving subjective feelings, cognitive appraisal, physiological arousal, and behavioral tendencies. Emotion is an integral and complex aspect of human cognition that is decisive in human decision-making, behavior, and social interaction.

- Cognitive mechanism: Emotion generation involves cognitive appraisal (e.g., Lazarus's appraisal theory) - the brain evaluates the significance and relevance of a stimulus, which triggers the corresponding emotional response. The dimensional model (valence–arousal) and discrete emotion theories (Ekman's basic emotions) are two major cognitive frameworks.
- Neural mechanism: The generation of human emotions is highly correlated with electrical signals in the cerebral cortex of the brain. Key structures include the amygdala (rapid emotional evaluation, especially fear), prefrontal cortex (emotion regulation and appraisal), insular cortex (interoception and subjective feeling), and anterior cingulate cortex (conflict monitoring). The activity of different brain regions was closely related to some kind of emotional states.

1. DGCNN (Dynamic Graph Convolutional Neural Network)
   A novel dynamic graph convolutional neural network (DGCNN) model was proposed, which simultaneously optimized the network parameters and a weighted graph G characterizing the strength of functional relation between each pair of two electrodes. The basic idea is to use a graph to model the multichannel EEG features and then perform EEG emotion classification based on this model. It learns inter-channel topology adaptively via backpropagation.
2. RGNN (Regularized Graph Neural Network)
   RGNN considers the biological topology among different brain regions to capture both local and global relations among different EEG channels. Specifically, it models the inter-channel relations via an adjacency matrix in a graph neural network where the connection and sparseness are inspired by neuroscience theories of human brain organization. It also introduces node-wise domain adversarial training for cross-subject robustness.
3. BiLSTM-based EEG Emotion Recognition
   This method uses a long short-term memory (LSTM)-based approach that selects window sizes and overlaps to segment EEG data, and adds a Bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) layer to standard LSTM layers to better detect forward and backward patterns in the data, improving both feature extraction and classification accuracy. It captures temporal dependencies in EEG sequences end-to-end.

- DGCNN / SparseDGCNN Example)
  The sparse DGCNN model modifies DGCNN by imposing a sparseness constraint on G and improves the emotion recognition performance, based on the observation that different brain regions may be related to different functions and the functional relations among electrodes are possibly highly localized and sparse.
  On the SEED dataset, DGCNN achieved ~90.4% average accuracy for three-class emotion classification; SparseDGCNN further improved this. On DEAP, the BiLSTM approach reported: arousal (94.0%), liking (98.9%), dominance (95.3%), and valence (99.6%).
- Proposed Improvements
  1. Multi-scale temporal fusion: DGCNN captures spatial (graph) features well but one limitation is its inability to capture temporal features since EEG is a time-serial signal. Combining DGCNN with temporal models (e.g., Transformer or LSTM) in a hybrid architecture would jointly model spatial and temporal dynamics.
  2. Cross-subject domain adaptation: Individual EEG variability degrades generalization. Adding domain adversarial training or self-supervised contrastive pre-training (as in RGNN) can reduce subject-specific distribution shifts. Contrastive learning can improve the quality of learned representations and mitigate the adverse impact of inter-subject and intra-subject variability.
  3. Attention-guided sparse graph learning: Instead of a fixed sparseness constraint, use a learnable multi-head attention mechanism to dynamically weight electrode connections per frequency band, improving interpretability and performance. Integrating time-frequency features with Bi-GRU and self-attention mechanisms improves recognition accuracy.
  4. Data augmentation: GANs have shown notable data augmentation success; leveraging GAN-based techniques can enhance robustness by synthetically increasing dataset size.
