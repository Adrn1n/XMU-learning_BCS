1. 
	- Differences
		Real neurons are alive, chemical, noisy, fire timed spikes, and reshape naturally. In contrast, the three classic ANN models - Perceptron (single binary decision), MLP (Multi-Layer Perceptron, stacked layers for complex patterns), and CNN (Convolutional Neural Network, shared filters for spatial features) - are purely mathematical, deterministic, and lack the biological plasticity, timing, and chemistry of real neurons.
	- Similarities
		Despite these differences, both biological neurons and ANN neurons collect multiple inputs, sum them, and decide whether to "fire" an output based on a threshold. All three classic models implement this core mechanism: Perceptron does it for simple decisions, MLP extends it across layers for complex patterns, and CNN applies it with filters for spatial feature extraction.
2. 
	- Frontier progress
		Scientists now use AI plus new tools like light microscopy with tissue expansion (called LICONN, 2025) and high-resolution electron microscopy to build detailed 3D maps of thousands of neurons and millions of connections. Examples include a full cubic-millimeter piece of human brain (Harvard/Google, 2024) and whole-mouse cortex neuron maps (2025-2026). AI speeds up tracing and finds hidden links that old methods missed.
	- How it helps
		These 3D maps show the exact shape, branches, and real wiring between neurons at tiny scale. We can see new cell types, rare strong connections, and how signals flow in living circuits. This gives a clearer picture of brain computation, learning, and memory—far better than flat 2D slices—helping explain normal function and diseases.
